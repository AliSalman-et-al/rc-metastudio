Aspirin for Preventing Death after Myocardial Infarction: random-effects meta-analysis (odds ratio)

Comparison: Aspirin / Control

Methods

Statistical analyses were performed using RC MetaStudio (version 0.6.0; Research Consultancy) [1] with the R package metafor (version 5.2.1) [2]. Odds ratios were pooled using a random-effects model with the restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimator of between-study variance [3]. Confidence intervals (95%) used the normal approximation. The meta-regression model included Subgroup. Regression coefficients and their confidence intervals are on the log scale. Heterogeneity was assessed with Cochran’s Q and I².

Results

In total, 6 studies were included (5,599 participants in Aspirin, 5,217 in Control); 1 study was excluded. For Subgroup: Later trials vs Earlier trials (reference), the coefficient was 0.28 (95% CI −0.03 to 0.58; z = 1.79; P = 0.074). Residual heterogeneity: Q = 4.25 (df = 4; P = 0.373); I² = 22.15%; τ² = 0.01. In Earlier trials (reference), the pooled odds ratio was 0.75 (95% CI 0.59 to 0.93). In Later trials, the pooled odds ratio was 0.98 (95% CI 0.80 to 1.21).
