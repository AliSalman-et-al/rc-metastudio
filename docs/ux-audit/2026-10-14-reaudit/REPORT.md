# RC MetaStudio native re-audit — 14 October 2026

## 1. Executive summary

Round two is a substantial improvement over the [13 October re-audit](../2026-10-13-reaudit/REPORT.md). Reports reflow, numerical results have real tables, subgroup comparisons are available in the report, diagnostic values retain their leading digits, and results can be re-run with their settings restored. The default forest now has conventional metric headings, weights, available raw arm quantities, a bold pooled estimate and separate statistical footers. This is much closer to a usable research product.

Publication readiness still requires checking the output rather than accepting the default. The most serious new regression is incorrect HR/IRR leave-one-out figure labels: an omission fit is labelled **All studies** and subsequent study names shift. Fixed-effect forests also present **τ² = 0** as if between-study variance had been estimated. Configuration labels can describe inactive estimators, and copied/exported report bodies lose outcome/comparison identity when it is moved into the surrounding window header. No critical defect or new numerical fitting error was established.

## 3. Regressions introduced by round two

### R2-01 — High: HR/IRR omission figures assign the wrong study to the estimate

The HR [figure](screenshots/matrix-hr-leave-one-out-figure.png) labels **0.886845 [0.798637, 0.984795]** as **All studies**, then assigns **0.882649** to Study 1. The [report](screenshots/matrix-hr-leave-one-out-report.png) correctly assigns the former to omission of Study 1 and the latter to omission of Study 2. The full random-effects estimate is **0.861873**, visible in the [cumulative final row](screenshots/matrix-hr-cumulative-report.png) and [subgroup Overall row](screenshots/matrix-hr-subgroup-report.png). IRR repeats the mismatch: [figure](screenshots/matrix-irr-leave-one-out-figure.png), [report](screenshots/matrix-irr-leave-one-out-report.png), [Overall](screenshots/matrix-irr-subgroup-report.png). The last study name is lost rather than adding an actual full-data row. The [13 October HR figure](../2026-10-13-reaudit/screenshots/matrix-hr-leave-one-out-figure.png) correctly attached these same values to the omission labels.

**Principle:** statistical identity must survive presentation changes. **Recommendation:** make the shared sequential renderer distinguish an omission-only vector from a vector containing an overall fit. Either prepend the actual full fit explicitly or keep omission-only labels; never infer an overall fit from position. Verify row name, estimate and CI together on HR and IRR, including excluded/non-estimable studies. Source corroboration: `ratio_methods.R` supplies omission-only fits, while `forest_metafor.R` assumes the first fit is overall. [Retained independent comparisons](omission-proof.json) pass for the underlying omission results; they do not validate these plotted labels.

### R2-02 — High: external report bodies lose their clinical identity

The [live report](screenshots/21-fixed-report.png) identifies outcome/type/comparison in the window header, but its document begins with configuration and does not contain those identity lines. [Copy report feedback](screenshots/22-copy-feedback.png) confirms the action. The actual native UX-helper [exported text](inputs/exported-analysis.txt) begins with project/model and omits the outcome “clinical failure” and follow-up “first” visible in its [result header](screenshots/suite-review-workflows-06-0-review-report-top.png). A project filename alone is insufficient when one project contains multiple outcomes, comparisons and follow-ups. The [earlier report](../2026-10-13-reaudit/screenshots/matrix-hr-leave-one-out-report.png) included identity inside its document.

**Principle:** exported artifacts must make sense independently. **Recommendation:** keep the requested config-first on-screen layout, but prefix copied/exported reports with analysis, outcome, data type/metric, comparison/group and follow-up. Use the same result-owned snapshot as the visible header. Do not require a screenshot of the application to identify a text report.

### R2-03 — Medium: text export flattens tables into one cell per line

The real [export opened in native Notepad](screenshots/41-exported-report-native.png) lists τ², Q, df, P and I² on separate lines, then lists their values on separate lines below. Even the single pooled estimate and its P value lose their adjacent row layout. The [on-screen table](screenshots/suite-review-workflows-06-0-review-report-top.png) is much easier to read. The earlier [copied report payload](../2026-10-13-reaudit/copied-report.txt), with [native report evidence](../2026-10-13-reaudit/screenshots/matrix-hr-leave-one-out-report.png), kept labelled numerical rows together; replacing its body with HTML cells while exporting `toPlainText()` sacrifices that structure. Numbers are retained, but pairing and scanning are worse, especially for multirow tables.

**Principle:** preserve relationships when changing representation. **Recommendation:** serialize the existing structured table rows as readable aligned text or tab-separated rows for plain-text export/copy. Keep wrapping paragraphs and add the identity from R2-02. Rich clipboard HTML can supplement a useful plain-text fallback; native table CSV already provides a good focused route.

## 4. New and remaining findings

### F01 — High: a fixed-effect forest implies estimated zero heterogeneity

The [generic fixed forest](screenshots/20-fixed-forest.png), [HR forest](screenshots/matrix-hr-standard-figure.png) and [IRR forest](screenshots/matrix-irr-standard-figure.png) print **τ² = 0.00/0.000000** under a fixed-effect model. A fixed-effect assumption is not an estimate demonstrating absence of between-study variance. Q and I² may still describe heterogeneity; a zero τ² line is misleading in this context. The corresponding [fixed report](screenshots/matrix-hr-standard-report.png) appropriately omits τ², so the two presentations disagree.

**Principle:** distinguish assumptions from estimates. **Recommendation:** omit τ² from fixed-effect footers; retain appropriate Q, df, P and I². Only print estimated τ² for a model that estimates it. This matches the established RevMan distinction: [Cochrane's RevMan change documentation](https://training.cochrane.org/sites/training.cochrane.org/files/public/uploads/resources/Handbook5_1/Whats%20new%20in%20Handbook%205_0.pdf) describes τ² at the beginning of the random-effects heterogeneity line. [Cochrane Handbook chapter 10](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-10) explains the assumptions of fixed and random models. This audit does not establish when the defect was first introduced.

### F02 — High: configuration lists inactive or ambiguous estimators

Changing the [generic setup](screenshots/19-fixed-setup.png) from random to fixed produces a [fixed report](screenshots/21-fixed-report.png) that still says **Estimator: DerSimonian–Laird**. The fixed method form has no such selected estimator. HR and IRR fixed reports reproduce it without relying on the manual transition. In [diagnostic setup](screenshots/matrix-diagnostic-standard-setup.png), DL is selected, yet the [report](screenshots/matrix-diagnostic-standard-report.png) lists DL and REML under each metric. The metric association is better than before, but the configuration is still not an unambiguous account of the actual fit.

**Principle:** trustworthy provenance and recognition. **Recommendation:** derive the summary from the executed model's applicable settings, suppress inactive parameters, and identify each diagnostic component once with its actual estimator/inference. Test fixed↔random re-runs and mixed diagnostic methods. No incorrect numerical fit is inferred from these labels.

### F03 — Medium: reports omit statistics already visible in their figures

The [one-arm mean subgroup report](screenshots/matrix-onearm-mean-subgroup-report.png) and [SMD report](screenshots/matrix-twoarm-smd-subgroup-report.png) provide estimates and a subgroup-comparison test but not the same k, within-group heterogeneity and PI information shown in their [figures](screenshots/matrix-onearm-mean-subgroup-figure.png) / [footer](screenshots/matrix-onearm-mean-subgroup-bottom.png). Binary/diagnostic reports have richer subgroup table shapes. Sequential reports use **Studies**, **Overall** and `+ / −` rows while the figures use **Study added (k)** and **All studies / Study omitted**. HR/IRR omission reports correctly contain omission-only fits and should explicitly say so.

**Principle:** consistent information across views. **Recommendation:** carry existing fitted k, heterogeneity, available PI and named row roles into the structured report tables. Give the final cumulative/all-studies summary an explicit scope. Reuse package outputs; do not invent additional tests or prediction intervals. Keep the subgroup-difference test distinct from within-subgroup P values.

### F04 — Medium: regression axes and precision still vary in quality

The [binary bubble plot](screenshots/matrix-twoarm-proportion-meta-regression-figure.png) places many y ticks in a small band of the full displayed CI range. Other [HR](screenshots/matrix-hr-meta-regression-figure.png) and [IRR](screenshots/matrix-irr-meta-regression-figure.png) axes are much more legible. Comparative [MD cumulative](screenshots/matrix-twoarm-mean-cumulative-figure.png) and [HR omission](screenshots/matrix-hr-leave-one-out-figure.png) plots lack the vertical null reference used by the ordinary forest. A [coefficient forest](screenshots/matrix-reg-coef-standard-figure.png) exposes a **1.110e−16** CI bound where the fixture's entered bound is zero: preserving floating-point residue is not useful clinical precision. Useful genuinely small bounds are now retained, which is an improvement.

**Principle:** readable uncertainty, meaningful precision. **Recommendation:** choose tick density over the full plotted range using rendered label spacing; show and identify an in-range 0/1 null reference for comparative sequential plots. Suppress representational round-off at a documented presentation tolerance while preserving genuinely small nonzero estimates. Apply the selected precision consistently; avoid making six decimals the default publication style merely because the stored input has them.

### F05 — Medium: the overall-effect null is not explicit for diagnostic/single-arm data

The [specificity figure](screenshots/matrix-diagnostic-cumulative-figure-1.png) prominently reports **Test for overall effect: Z = 13.677 (P < 0.001)** without naming the tested null. On the selected transformed scale this is not a general verdict that a diagnostic test is clinically useful. Single-arm [mean](screenshots/matrix-onearm-mean-standard-figure.png) and [proportion](screenshots/matrix-onearm-proportion-standard-figure.png) outputs likewise need a named null rather than an unqualified success-looking test line.

**Principle:** prevent overinterpretation. **Recommendation:** name the tested value and scale using actual method metadata, or de-emphasize that line when the default null has little clinical relevance. Keep sensitivity/specificity, uncertainty and clinical cautions primary. Do not alter the fit or invent a clinically meaningful threshold.

### F06 — Medium: calculator density and grid allocation still add friction

The [continuous calculator](screenshots/suite-calculator-continuous-03-0-calculator-effect-preview.png) still requires vertical and horizontal scrolling and abbreviates effect/estimate labels; binary [preview](screenshots/suite-calculator-binary-03-0-calculator-effect-preview.png) has an edge-clipped Back-Calculate action. Diagnostic values are now complete. The manual [imported grid](screenshots/11-imported-grid.png), using persisted ordinary preferences, gives a short category column most of the empty width while study names get much less. Fresh helper grids distribute spare room better; this is not called an initial auto-sizing regression. Red selection remains strong even though the new local inclusion/derived-cell explanation helps.

**Principle:** recognition, readable targets, economical layout. **Recommendation:** show only the active calculator input mode at once, use existing arm names consistently, reserve space for its primary result/actions, and cap initially allocated category widths while respecting user resizing. This is layout work on existing calculators, not a new feature family.

### F07 — Low: terminology and help have not caught up with the workflows

The [covariate dialog](screenshots/33-add-covariate-empty.png) says **continuous** while nearby [guidance](screenshots/32-covariates.png) says **Numeric/Category**; its [context menu](screenshots/40-covariate-context-modal1.png) still says **Create a *factor* Copy**. Plot options use `[default]`, generic CSV schema uses **TX Mean** while the grid says **Estimate**, and the [diagnostic small-study header](screenshots/matrix-small-study-diagnostic-standard-figure.png) prints **None** where a metric should be. Menu casing/ellipses vary, and [Quick start](screenshots/36-quick-start.png) omits Tables, Change settings and display ordering. These are small but repeated translation costs.

**Principle:** one concept, one name; task-focused help. **Recommendation:** use the existing plain vocabulary throughout, distinguish cumulative sequence from display order in help, and document the new result toolbar with a short route to copy/export and re-run.

### Statistical and aesthetic assessment

The default binary/continuous forests now resemble the useful RevMan convention: actual metric, available Events/Total or Mean/SD/Total, percentage weights, bold summary diamond, separate Q/df/P/I², overall Z/t test and available PI. In the [six-study binary fixture](screenshots/matrix-twoarm-proportion-standard-figure.png), visible counts sum to **39/135 versus 51/147**, matching the displayed totals. [Continuous totals](screenshots/matrix-twoarm-mean-standard-figure.png) are **135/147**. SMD [report](screenshots/matrix-twoarm-smd-standard-report.png) and [figure](screenshots/matrix-twoarm-smd-standard-figure.png) agree at **0.233 [−0.002, 0.467], P = 0.052**. A random model estimating τ² at zero can legitimately have PI equal to CI; that is different from F01's fixed assumption. Diagnostic sensitivity and specificity remain separately labelled, with raw TP/FP/FN/TN and actual moderator names. These checks concern selected outputs, not every model combination.

Automatic finite forest CIs now remain visible in inspected standard/sequential/subgroup paths. Explicit user limits deliberately clip with arrows; direction labels are separate editable fields and blank by default, which avoids inventing benefit direction. A direction label needs the investigator's outcome/comparison meaning. Ratio axes identify their log scale and neutral value; MD/SMD explanations define subtraction and units. HR/IRR forests still omit available event/exposure columns and standalone outcome/person-time context. Long subgroup figures shrink text much more than short standard/sequential figures and need scrolling. Duplicate weights/reference sections add length after the essential output.

Compared with RevMan, RCMS has gained most of the basic reading grammar, but footer assumptions, precision and cross-view consistency still need polish. Compared with standard metafor/meta output, RCMS's tables and named moderators remove substantial decoding; free-form package printouts remain on some paths. Stata `metan`, CMA and JASP are useful design comparators for compact estimates, clear nulls and model details, but were not installed or driven side by side here. No numerical superiority or exact current-product equivalence is claimed. The [round-two benchmark sources](../2026-10-14-round2/BENCHMARKS.md) provide the recorded comparison context.
